Best Gifts for Rock Music Fans: 12 Ideas for 2026

The safest gift for a rock fan is a landmark album on vinyl, such as Pink Floyd’s The Dark Side of the Moon (1973) or Fleetwood Mac’s Rumours (1977). If they already own the records, give a music-history book like Please Kill Me; if they have no record player yet, a beginner turntable with a built-in preamp, such as the Audio-Technica AT-LP60X, plays records through powered speakers without a separate amplifier.

Landmark albums on vinyl

A record is the easiest rock gift to get right when you do not know a collection. These five are albums with a documented history, so you can say something true about them when you hand them over.

The Dark Side of the Moon (Pink Floyd) on vinyl

Pink Floyd’s eighth studio album came out on 1 March 1973 on Capitol Records in the United States and on 16 March on Harvest Records in the United Kingdom. It is a concept album about the pressures of life on the road, and it also deals with the mental health of former band member Syd Barrett.

Rumours (Fleetwood Mac) on vinyl

Fleetwood Mac’s eleventh studio album was released on 4 February 1977 by Warner Bros. Records. It was largely recorded in California in 1976, while band members were going through breakups with one another, and that turmoil shaped the lyrics.

See it on Amazon

Led Zeppelin IV on vinyl

Led Zeppelin’s untitled fourth album, known as Led Zeppelin IV, was released on 8 November 1971 by Atlantic Records. Guitarist Jimmy Page produced it, and it was recorded mostly at the country house Headley Grange. It includes the eight-minute “Stairway to Heaven.”

See it on Amazon

Nevermind (Nirvana) on vinyl

Nirvana’s second studio album arrived on 24 September 1991 on DGC Records. Butch Vig produced it, and it was the first Nirvana album to feature drummer Dave Grohl.

See it on Amazon

Back in Black (AC/DC) on vinyl

AC/DC’s seventh studio album was released on 25 July 1980. It was the first with singer Brian Johnson, who joined after the death of Bon Scott in February 1980.

Music-history books

For fans who already own the records, a book about how the music was made is a better gift than a fifth copy of the same album.

Please Kill Me (Legs McNeil and Gillian McCain)

Please Kill Me: The Uncensored Oral History of Punk by Legs McNeil and Gillian McCain was first published by Grove Press in 1996. It is told as an oral history, in the words of the people who were there.

See it on Amazon

Our Band Could Be Your Life (Michael Azerrad)

Michael Azerrad’s Our Band Could Be Your Life (Little, Brown, 2001) covers the American indie underground of 1981 to 1991 and profiles 13 bands, including Black Flag and the Minutemen.

See it on Amazon

Hammer of the Gods (Stephen Davis)

Stephen Davis’s Hammer of the Gods (1985) is an unauthorized biography of Led Zeppelin. Davis, then a Rolling Stone journalist, travelled with the band for two weeks at the start of its 1975 U.S. tour.

See it on Amazon

Life (Keith Richards)

Keith Richards’s memoir Life, written with journalist James Fox, was published in October 2010. It follows him from his childhood influences through the founding of the Rolling Stones and his turbulent relationship with Mick Jagger.

See it on Amazon

Quick answers

What is a good gift for a rock fan who has no record player?

A book from the second list or a pair of closed-back headphones works without any other equipment. If you do want to start them on vinyl, a turntable with a built-in preamp such as the AT-LP60X only needs powered speakers.

Does a beginner turntable need a separate preamp?

Not when it has a built-in, switchable preamp. Audio-Technica’s manual says to set the switch to LINE for equipment without a phono input and to PHONO for equipment that has one.

Is a record a safe gift if I do not know their collection?

Choose a landmark album rather than a greatest-hits set, and check that the listing names the artist, the album and the vinyl format. If you can, look at their shelf first to avoid a duplicate.
